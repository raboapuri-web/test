# 「考える夜」 video direction

Treat a supplied script, runtime, aspect ratio, and reference images as the creative brief. The following are series defaults for gaps in that brief.

- Aim for thoughtful, cinematic visuals: near-black backgrounds, ivory Japanese typography, muted gold emphasis, subtle grain and vignette. Use the existing sample's palette as a starting point.
- Move to a new visual idea roughly every 3–7 seconds. Let the narration's argument determine exact cuts. Give diagrams enough time to read.
- Vary illustration, motion graphics, diagrams, and typography. For a long-form video, start near 50/25/15/10 percent respectively, then adjust to the script instead of forcing quotas on a short sample.
- Use slow zoom, pan, or layered depth for still artwork; avoid long static holds and repetitive slide layouts. Animate with Remotion frames so Studio and renders agree.
- Show only key Japanese words or short questions, usually no more than 14 characters per caption. Do not turn the entire narration into on-screen text unless the brief asks for subtitles.
- Prefer original or licensed imagery and audio. If a sample has no supplied voice or assets, use code-native visuals and label it as a silent prototype.
- Verify typography, safe margins, timing, and factual labels in a rendered sample. Where a claim needs a source, keep the source in production notes rather than on screen unless the brief asks otherwise.

Read the relevant official Remotion Skill in the sibling `remotion-*` folders before implementing Remotion details.
